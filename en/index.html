The method

How I read a hotel

A hotel is not judged by its photographs. It is judged by the people who work there. The four things I check for you are the four I check here every morning, from behind the desk.

  1. Who is at the desk. I ask how many years they have been there. The answer says more than the declared category.
  2. Whether the faces come back. Next season you find the same people, or you do not. No hotel writes that in its own presentation.
  3. Who can say yes. If nobody on the floor decides anything without asking upstairs, by the fifth day you feel it.
  4. What happens when something goes wrong. The level of a place is not visible on arrival. It is visible there.

It is the part photographs never tell you, and the part that stays with you.

This way of looking has a name and a score. I turned it into a tool: 23 questions about observable facts, no reviews, a score from 0 to 100. Three minutes, and you can measure the last hotel you slept in yourself.
